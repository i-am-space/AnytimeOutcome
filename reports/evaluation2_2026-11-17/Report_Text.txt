Why the original outputs differed
Single-query and batched execution used different numerical paths. BF16 rounding and
reduction-order differences can change nearly tied logits, flip the next greedy token, and
then change the rest of the response. The original implementation also exhibited
discrepancies.
What we changed
AR, SD and SSD use shared fixed-shape target arithmetic and batch-independent
attention. The shared AR reference is versioned explicitly. It can generate different words
from original AR; matching it does not repair historical-original-AR equivalence.

The problem
Complete-cache SSD can leave the target waiting while the drafter finishes work that
is only partly needed. Our project asks whether safely publishing ready prefixes and
assigning unequal branch depths can reduce full-request latency.
What the measurements establish
On this L40S setup, target decode/verify is mostly memory-bound. The SSD trace
shows 9.2 ms of target waiting on cache hits and 30.0 ms on misses. Five of seven
draft depths are ready in 155 of 166 hit rounds.
What is still open
The anytime scheduler has no measured results yet. Fast SD and SSD differ from the
unchanged AR reference on all three saved prompts. A slow synchronous reference
matches 768 AR tokens, but fast correctness remains unresolved.
Included
Machine roofline, model-width sweeps,
AR/SD/SSD Nsight traces, five Nsight Compute
phase captures, first-divergence controls,
backend isolation, numerical reference, and
harness validation.
New results
All 480 tuning measurements are complete; SD
K6 and SSD K7/F3 are frozen winners. AR and
SSD each have one valid 75-prompt held-out
round. A separate 75-prompt SD GPU0-only round
is complete and labeled contextual. Five-repeat
held-out validation is incomplete.
