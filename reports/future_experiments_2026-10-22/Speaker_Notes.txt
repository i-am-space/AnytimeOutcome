SLIDE 1: Future experimentation plan
Scope: all remaining project experiments, based on the uploaded two-page proposal. This presentation is a plan, not a new results report. The user requested removal of a dedicated named fan-out comparator; the comparisons shown follow the proposal. Unequal fan-out alone is established work and is not claimed as our contribution. The proposed contribution concerns incremental publication and unequal continuation depths, judged against simple early handoff.

SLIDE 2: What is done, and what remains?
Current status is grounded in the saved baseline status and the immutable completed-results snapshot. All 480 tuning requests are valid. Dedicated held-out evidence consists of 75 AR and 75 SSD fixed-length requests, one repetition each. A separate 75-request SD run on clean GPU0 with GPU1 occupied is contextual and does not replace a dedicated campaign repetition. The two-hour window ended with the campaign incomplete. Profiling and microbenchmarks exist. Fast-mode exact agreement with unchanged AR still fails; the slow serial diagnostic reference is not an optimized correctness fix.

SLIDE 3: The remaining work has four stages
Correctness work and completion of unprofiled baseline measurements can proceed as separate tracks. No correctness-preserving acceleration claim is allowed until the unchanged target outputs match. After uniform early publication, continue the adaptive scheduler only if measured headroom remains. The timeline is ordered by evidence gates, not a promise of GPU availability or calendar dates.

SLIDE 4: What will we compare?
AR and synchronous SD are context controls using one GPU; SSD-based policy comparisons use the same two-GPU target/drafter allocation. Fixed global depth is covered by K tuning. Add an online global depth/fan-out comparator to test whether simple adaptation is sufficient. Uniform early publication with immediate handoff is the key comparator. Unequal fixed continuation depths isolate allocation. Probability-first and SwiftSpec-inspired timing policies are same-executor adaptations, not claims to reproduce their entire original systems. Candidate construction and ranking stay fixed initially. Match candidate/work budgets, memory constraints, hardware, and tuning effort across policy comparisons.

SLIDE 5: First gate: reproduce the target exactly
The reference is the unchanged original target-only path. Investigate numerical shape effects under identical committed histories and distinguish rounding evidence from a fix. Test rejection at first and later tokens, all-accepted bonus handling, EOS in every relevant position, output caps, partial prefix use, and branch KV reconciliation. Stress round/generation IDs, stale buffers, buffer reuse, completion events, and in-flight kernel drain. The slow serial reference is diagnostic evidence only. If the fast shared executor changes, create a new execution fingerprint and rerun/reselect affected baselines; do not splice old timings into a new campaign.

SLIDE 6: Experiment 1: hand over a ready prefix
Diagram is illustrative and contains no measured timing or predicted speedup. A continuation with four completed tokens may be useful even if the maximum intended depth is seven. Publish only after a CUDA completion event confirms writes; advertise an immutable valid length tied to round, branch, and generation IDs. At handoff use the actual outcome's completed prefix, or a fixed target-only fallback. Stop obsolete launches and include drain costs. Tune publication checkpoints such as 1, 2, 4, and 8 only where the configured depth permits; K=7 cannot publish depth 8.

SLIDE 7: Experiment 2: give outcomes different depths
Outcome means an accepted-prefix length plus recovery/bonus token. The allocation diagram is an illustration, not a new result. Both examples use 12 draft token-steps, but equal logical work does not imply equal GPU runtime because batch shapes differ. Measure execution cost and constrain the same budget when comparing policies. Start with fixed unequal depths. Then use outcome frequencies, acceptance statistics, measured increment costs, and readiness estimates to choose which branch to extend. Bound controller work and revert to a baseline with sparse statistics. Unequal fan-out and adaptive drafting are existing ideas; the project must demonstrate benefit from publication and depth scheduling beyond the simpler comparators.

SLIDE 8: Ablations: what actually causes a gain?
These are planned end-to-end experiments in the shared executor, with fair retuning on separate tuning data. Measure actual policy overhead. The first comparison isolates publication; the second isolates allocation; the third isolates online adaptation. Disabling readiness estimates isolates their value, while frozen versus online statistics tests learning. Profile controller, packing, synchronization and drain costs separately; an ideal zero-overhead controller is only a diagnostic upper bound, never a headline measured implementation.

SLIDE 9: Trade-offs: earlier is not always faster
Sweep publication checkpoints, maximum depth, candidate/fan-out budgets and execution-shape buckets on tuning data. Compare equal total work/candidate budgets where meaningful, report actual GPU cost and memory, and freeze winners before final evaluation. Earlier handoff can reduce target waiting but also reduce useful tokens per verification. Frequent publication adds events, packing and synchronization. More branches increase coverage and consume memory or draft work. A controller can improve choices yet lose end-to-end after its decision overhead. Record discarded/obsolete draft token work and actual kernel-drain time.

SLIDE 10: Final workloads: test beyond the first 75 prompts
The proposal targets 100 held-out prompts per family, 300 total. These are not 100 per context length and do not automatically imply a fully crossed 900-prompt grid. Use complete natural prompts around 512, 2K and 8K tokens where available; report coverage gaps without inventing or silently padding inputs. Record exact source IDs, splits, tokenizer/chat template and token IDs. Keep the new final held-out set separate from tuning and previously inspected campaign examples. Output caps are 128 and 512 with normal EOS; exact fixed-length throughput is supplementary. Current capacity is 4096: 8K inputs need a separately fingerprinted longer-capacity configuration with output headroom, memory checks and matching baselines. Do not truncate them into a claimed 8K test.

SLIDE 11: Measure user experience and explain the cause
Full-request timing includes both prefills, verification, waiting, fallback, controller/publication and necessary drain. Exclude model loading, compilation, warmup, logging and serialization. Report mean and p50/p95 request latency; first committed output; p50/p95 gaps between real committed deliveries; committed tokens/s with denominator defined; and peak GPU memory. Delivery gaps and peak memory need added instrumentation beyond the old campaign summary. Diagnostic metrics: actual outcome, ready-prefix coverage and depth at handoff, accepted tokens per verification, target waiting/slowdown, fallback and cache-hit rates, discarded work, controller overhead and drain. Repeat per-round hit/miss and GPU-completion analysis in separate profiles; the new unprofiled campaign did not record that waiting breakdown.

SLIDE 12: Keep the comparisons controlled
Use warm runs with at least five repetitions for every headline setting; the existing campaign uses three separate warmup requests for each fresh process/configuration block. Alternate or seed method order, preserve prompt pairing, and declare the workload aggregation before results. Use 95% paired confidence intervals; the existing analysis supports 10000 seeded hierarchical bootstrap draws over rounds and prompts within families. All methods share weights, precision, backend, capacity, token IDs and EOS rules. Prefix lookup stays disabled so scored requests perform full prefill. SSD policy comparisons match both GPU allocation and tuning budget. Pin GPU UUIDs, record environment hashes, monitor unrelated processes, invalidate contaminated blocks and resume only completed compatible evidence. Never blend different execution versions.

SLIDE 13: Optimize on L40S, then check portability
Primary disaggregated experiments use the actual two L40S GPUs on the same server, across NUMA sockets over PCIe without NVLink. Do not inherit the proposal's unverified topology wording. After correctness, evaluate CUDA streams/events, preallocated buffers and execution-shape buckets. Add CUDA/Triton packing kernels or further CUDA graph capture only if profiles justify them. A/B measure correctness, latency, publication/control overhead, target interference and memory. Proposal lists only one RTX3090: use it for fitting, microbenchmarks or single-device controls; do not present those as replicated two-GPU SSD. Full SSD replication is conditional on a valid second device and documented topology. RTX2080 FP16 smaller-model tests are supplementary, subject to access; they do not replace main-model correctness.

SLIDE 14: Milestones are evidence gates
These gates update the proposal's week-based milestones without claiming missed weeks are done. Evaluation1 covers baseline status, unresolved correctness, bottleneck evidence and experimental design. Before Evaluation2 aim for a safe uniform early-publication prototype and, only if it has residual headroom, unequal-depth/adaptive scheduling with preliminary ablations. Final evaluation requires frozen held-out tests, repeated timings, confidence intervals, regressions and reproducible artifacts. If adaptive scheduling fails to improve on the simple early-handoff policy after its overhead, retain the correct simpler system and quantify the negative result.

SLIDE 15: What the finished project must demonstrate
The proposal engineering target is at least 10% lower mean full-request latency on the declared balanced held-out mix than the strongest matched tested baseline; report paired uncertainty and every family regression. This is a target, not a forecast or guaranteed win. Correctness requires token-for-token greedy equality to unchanged target AR, not tolerance-aware logit checks. Deliver runnable code, manifest/configuration versions, raw measurements, plots/traces, report, presentation and demo. Proposed ownership: Ishaan baseline integration, KV/handoff and correctness; Vishal scheduling, statistics and ablations; Eshaan publication, execution optimization and profiling. Single-request greedy decoding remains core scope; stochastic sampling and multi-request serving are outside the required experiments.
